## Segment 4: Round 2 & The Nash Trap (5:30 – 8:30)

*[CUE: Anushka clicks `NEXT`. Round 2 begins: 25-second timer.]*

**ANUSHKA:**  
"Round two is live! Twenty-five seconds! You have seen the room's data. You know people understand Level-1 thinking. Adapt and lock it in!"

*[STAGE: Anushka watches the submitted counter. When all players submit or timer expires, auto-reveal fires.]*

*[SCREEN: Comparative dual histograms appear showing Round 1 vs Round 2 side-by-side.]*

**ADITI:**  
"Look at the screen! Round 1 average was **[read R1 Mean]**. Round 2 average plummeted to **[read R2 Mean]**! The entire distribution just crashed to the left because everyone tried to undercut the room! But Visesh — where does this spiral actually end mathematically?"

*[CUE: Anushka clicks `NEXT` to show the Number Spiral & Nash Trap debrief slide.]*

**VISESH:**  
"It ends at a mathematical trap. Follow the logic step by step with me:  
Can any guess above 66.7 ever win? Think about it: even if every single person in this room went crazy and picked 100, the average would be 100, and two-thirds of that is 66.7! That means any number above 67 is **strictly dominated** — it is guaranteed to lose under every possible scenario. Cross it off!"

**VISESH:**  
"Now the spiral begins. Since every rational person eliminates numbers above 67, the new maximum possible average is 67. Two-thirds of 67 is 44.4! So anything above 44.4 is now guaranteed to lose. Cross that off too!  
Repeat this process:  
44.4 $\rightarrow$ 29.6 $\rightarrow$ 19.8 $\rightarrow$ 13.2 $\rightarrow$ 8.8 $\rightarrow$ all the way down to exactly one number: **ZERO**."

**VISESH:**  
"Zero is this game's unique **Nash Equilibrium**. If every single person picks zero, the average is zero, the target is zero, and nobody can unilaterally deviate to improve their score.  
**But here is the trap:** Did anyone in this room pick zero? If you picked zero today, you lost! Why? Because of **Bounded Rationality** (Herbert Simon). Textbooks assume humans have infinite cognitive depth. Real humans reason one or two steps ahead. In the real world, the hyper-rational player loses to the player who understands how rational the room actually is!"

**SARASWATI:**  
"Visesh, Atharv — was that rational?"

*[CUE: Anushka clicks `NEXT` to show the Payoff Matrix & Utility Theory debrief slide.]*

**VISESH:**  
*[Pointing at the 2x2 matrix on the screen]*  
"Let's look at the cold mathematical payoff matrix. This is the classic **Prisoner's Dilemma**.  
If your opponent splits: stealing gives you the whole prize instead of half. Stealing is better!  
If your opponent steals: stealing gives you zero, and splitting gives you zero. Stealing is never worse!  
In pure monetary terms, **STEAL is a weakly dominant strategy**. And when both players follow their dominant strategy, they both steal, destroying the prize. Rational individuals, irrational outcome!"

**ATHARV:**  
"So why do people ever split? In real classrooms and experiments, people split over 50% of the time! Why?  
Because of **Expected Utility Theory**: **Utility does not equal money!**  
In economics, your utility function $U$ includes your financial payout, minus your **guilt term**, minus your **reputation cost**, plus your satisfaction from **fairness**!  
If stealing from your classmate in front of thirty witnesses costs you more emotional distress and social stigma than half a prize is worth, **SPLITTING is the genuinely rational, utility-maximizing move**! Game theory isn't about being selfish — it's about maximizing what you truly value."

## Segment 8: Buffer & Professor Defense (14:30 – 15:00)

*[STAGE: Hold on the Wrap screen. Ready to field professor questions using the cheat-sheet below:]*

* **Q: "Why is 0 the Nash Equilibrium?"**  
  $\rightarrow$ **VISESH:** *"Iterated elimination of strictly dominated strategies: guesses $>66.7$ can never win. Pruning them caps the average at 66.7, making guesses $>44.4$ dominated. This induction contracts to the singleton set $\{0\}$."*
* **Q: "Why didn't the class guess 0?"**  
  $\rightarrow$ **ADITI:** *"Because equilibrium requires infinite common knowledge of rationality. Real humans operate at Level-1 or Level-2 depth. A guess of 0 against humans who average 35 will lose to a guess of 23."*
* **Q: "Why did the finalist choose to split?"**  
  $\rightarrow$ **ATHARV:** *"Payoffs are utils, not cash. In repeated classroom interactions, the social and reputational penalty of betrayal outweighs half a prize."*
